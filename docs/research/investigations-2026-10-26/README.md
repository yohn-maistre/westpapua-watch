## How the research fits together

These are organising collections, not a proposed menu:

- **Political access and public authority:** campaign finance, parties, appointments, legislation, permits, public procurement, autonomy and territorial administration.
- **Land, extraction and economic benefit:** mining, gas, plantations, food/energy estates, contractors, lenders, commodity buyers, public revenues and labour.
- **Security and coercion:** military business, security arrangements, weapons transfers, operations, detention, displacement and accountability.
- **Information and public debate:** internet restrictions, surveillance, coordinated influence, official statements, access to evidence and litigation.
- **Papuan political life:** organising, churches, customary authority, women's land/food knowledge, intellectual work, resistance, diplomacy and alternatives.
- **Historical formation:** decolonisation, Dutch/Indonesian/US/UN policy, Cold War decisions and the evolution of resource rights and institutions.

Each named investigation uses the same shared records. For example, Merauke can contain project decisions, a machine order, land instruments, testimony and a court proceeding. The ownership record of a participating company can also appear in a political-finance investigation, while keeping its date and evidence unchanged.

No allegation should become accepted merely because several dossiers reuse it. A sourced statement that someone made an allegation and a finding that the alleged act occurred are separate claims.

## What to do next

Take the first six dossiers in the brief through a bounded evidence pass. For each, produce:

1. One clear question, period and geographic scope.
2. An authored account of the supported sequence, outcomes and Papuan experience/action.
3. A dated actor/role record, a timeline and typed relationships.
4. Financial/material records with the amount, accounting basis and stage retained.
5. Source passages with page/paragraph/timestamp locators, responses and contradictions.
6. A document-specific gap list and update triggers.

Start with a small reviewed body of reusable claims. Decide the graph's layouts and interactions after testing what readers can actually learn from those claims. The user explicitly deferred UI specifics in this turn.
